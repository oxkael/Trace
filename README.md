TRACE

Understand the market before you make your move.

TRACE is a market research and briefing tool that turns a trader's questions into a clear explanation of what's happening in the market.

Ask a question in plain English. TRACE gathers the relevant market data, news, events, historical context, and available analyst views, then brings them together into a briefing that explains what matters, what the evidence suggests, and what remains uncertain.

Instead of making the decision for you, TRACE helps you understand the information behind it.

The problem

Market information is scattered.

A trader researching a stock might need to check its current price, understand whether the market is open, look for upcoming earnings, read several news articles, compare analyst opinions, and review recent price movements.

Each source provides a piece of the picture. Putting those pieces together takes time, and conflicting information can make the picture harder to understand.

Asking an AI chatbot is not always enough, either. An answer can sound convincing without being grounded in current data, or present an interpretation without making its supporting evidence clear.

TRACE brings relevant information together and explains how the pieces fit.

How it works

1. Ask a question. Ask about a stock, a market event, recent performance, upcoming earnings, or a possible price movement.
2. Gather the evidence. TRACE retrieves the relevant information available to it, including market prices, trading-session details, news, historical data, and analyst views where supported.
3. Build the briefing. An AI model interprets the collected information, connects relevant facts, explains competing perspectives, and identifies gaps in the evidence.
4. Make your own decision. Read the briefing, examine the supporting details, and decide what to do.

What makes TRACE different?

It starts with evidence

The model works from information assembled by the application rather than being asked to answer every market question from general knowledge alone. The aim is to ground explanations in identifiable data and make missing information explicit.

It explains context, not just numbers

A price movement means more when you understand the surrounding circumstances. Trading hours, upcoming events, recent performance, company news, and differing analyst views can all change how a trader interprets the same number.

TRACE brings relevant context into the same briefing.

It handles uncertainty honestly

Markets are uncertain, and available information can be incomplete or contradictory. TRACE should distinguish reported facts from interpretation, present meaningful disagreements, and explain what could not be verified.

It does not turn uncertainty into artificial confidence.

The trader stays in control

TRACE does not issue a final BUY, SELL, or WAIT verdict. It does not restrict trading based on its interpretation.

Its role is to explain the available evidence, not replace the trader's judgment.

What can you ask TRACE?

Examples of questions the product is designed to handle include:

- Market status: What's happening with NVDA right now? Is the market open?
- Upcoming events: What earnings announcements or other important events are coming up?
- Conflicting analysis: Why are analysts divided on this stock?
- Recent performance: What has driven the stock's recent movement?
- Forward-looking questions: What evidence supports or challenges the possibility that this stock will rise?
- Research before a trade: What should I understand about this stock and its current market conditions?

The goal is not to return the same template for every question. TRACE should identify the information relevant to the question and build its briefing around it.

Inside a briefing

A TRACE briefing is designed to be readable at a glance, with additional detail available when needed.

- Headline answer: A direct response to the question.
- Summary: The most relevant information in plain English.
- Supporting context: Expandable sections covering relevant prices, events, historical performance, news, and analyst views.
- Worth weighing: Important factors, competing explanations, and evidence that may support or challenge an interpretation.
- What is missing: Information that could not be found, verified, or established from the available sources.
- Glossary: Explanations of financial terms that may be unfamiliar to the reader.

The structure helps readers move from a quick understanding to a deeper examination of the evidence.

How the AI is used

AI is responsible for interpreting the question and explaining the evidence in a useful way. It is not a substitute for the underlying data.

The application retrieves and assembles relevant information, attaches explanations of what individual facts mean, and passes that context to the briefing model. The model then determines which details matter most to the question and explains their relationships.

The intended division of responsibility is straightforward:

- Data retrieval establishes what information is available.
- The briefing model explains the information and its significance.
- The trader evaluates the briefing and makes the decision.

This separation is central to TRACE's design.

Limitations

TRACE's explanations are only as reliable as the information available to it. Market data may be delayed, sources may disagree, and some questions may not have enough evidence for a firm conclusion.

A clear explanation is not a guarantee of accuracy, and historical performance does not establish future returns.

TRACE is a research aid, not a guarantee of market outcomes.

Project status

TRACE is under active development. The scope and availability of individual data sources and features depend on the current implementation.

The idea behind TRACE

A trader should not have to piece together an entire market story from disconnected sources before understanding what is happening.

TRACE is being built to make that process clearer: ask a question, examine the evidence, understand the context, and make your own decision.
